Caribbean - British Virgin Islands

Looking to unwind in paradise on earth, the Caribbean, aboard a catamaran? The British Virgin Islands, part of the Leeward Islands, are easily accessible from Central Europe in just about 9 hours by flight. Arriving via St. Martin, Puerto Rico, or Antigua is recommended. The connecting flight from St. Martin or Puerto Rico to the British Virgin Islands takes less than 45 minutes.

Once you arrive, you'll be captivated by the Caribbean way of life. Although driving is on the left, the islands' British formality is nowhere to be seen. Instead, you'll immerse yourself in the Caribbean’s art of relaxation, marveling and savoring every moment.

Turquoise waters lap around the over 60 islands of the British Virgin Islands, awaiting you on your Caribbean sailing adventure. Each island boasts its own unique character and beauty. Exploring the volcanic islands' many coastal miles aboard a catamaran offers a truly special experience.

As you glide through the crystal-clear waters, you'll notice that only a few of the islands are inhabited. The largest island, Tortola, is home to over 18,000 residents. The northern coast of Tortola enchants with its white sandy beaches, while the southern shores are lined with vibrant beach bars, inviting you to relax with colorful cocktails. The lively capital, Road Town, adds to the island's vibrant atmosphere.

Sailing Highlights of the British Virgin Islands

Tortola: The largest island in the British Virgin Islands boasts a stunning coastline with picturesque bays such as Cane Garden Bay and Brewers Bay. Known for its excellent sailing conditions, Tortola offers numerous anchorages in sheltered coves.

Virgin Gorda: Famous for the spectacular "Baths"—massive granite boulders creating stunning natural pools and beaches. Virgin Gorda is perfect for snorkeling and exploring its unique rock formations and hidden bays.

Jost Van Dyke: This laid-back island is renowned for its vibrant atmosphere, especially at the famous White Bay, where the legendary beach bar "Foxy's" is a popular gathering spot. Jost Van Dyke also offers great opportunities for water sports and relaxing beach days.

Anegada: The flattest of the islands, Anegada stands out with its pristine beaches and reefs. The surrounding coral reef is perfect for snorkeling and diving excursions. Anegada is also celebrated for its delicious lobster dishes.

Sailors enjoy the tropical mild temperatures of around 25°C in winter and 29°C in summer year-round on the British Virgin Islands. These temperatures are moderated by gentle breezes. The ideal travel period starts after the rainy season in December and lasts until the beginning of the hurricane season in June. The period with the least rainfall is between February and May. Throughout the year, beach lovers will find the Caribbean waters inviting, with average sea temperatures of 28°C.

In addition to cultural highlights, the British Virgin Islands offer excellent sailing conditions and a variety of activities that will make your sailing vacation unforgettable:

Caribbean Cuisine: Savor delicious Caribbean dishes such as Roti, Conch Fritters, fresh lobster, and other seafood specialties. Complement your meal with local rums and refreshing cocktails.

Culture and Traditions: Immerse yourself in the vibrant culture of the British Virgin Islands at festivals like the BVI Music Festival and the Festival of Traditional Arts. Visit historical sites such as the Tortola Museum and the ruins of Fort Charlotte.

Natural Experiences: Explore the pristine nature of the islands with hikes to scenic viewpoints or relax on stunning beaches like Cane Garden Bay and The Baths on Virgin Gorda.
