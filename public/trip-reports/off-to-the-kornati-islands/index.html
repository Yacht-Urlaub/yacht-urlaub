Off to the Kornati Islands!

A journey into Kornati National Park and the surrounding islands

Arrival Day – Anticipation Runs High

Friday afternoon, just after 2 p.m. — arrival in Biograd na Moru. Our seven-person crew, skipper included, reached this lovely Dalmatian town in the early afternoon. The sun was out and the mood was fantastic. Since the crew had decided to drive in a day ahead of the cruise's start, we settled comfortably into the hotel and used the time to acclimate.

Getting ready and switching off from everyday life topped the list for the day. "We brought fishing gear along, so we still need a license." Sorted in no time! The nearby tourist office made it quick and easy — a week's license runs about €50. Then some shopping at the usual stands — fins, dive masks, water shoes — everything your heart could want.

A little preview of the next day came with a walk over to the marina in Biograd, where the sailing yacht was already being prepped for departure. "Let's grab a bite. I know a good pizzeria," said one of the crew members — he'd sailed here before and knew Biograd well. This coastal town has plenty to offer night owls too, so after dinner nothing stood in the way of a relaxed bar visit. Then it was off to bed — a long day lay ahead.

Day 1 – Boarding the Yacht and Out to Sea!

Today's the day! After stocking up on provisions, the yacht was ready to board by early afternoon. The tanks were full and the drinks in the fridge were slowly reaching the right temperature. A quick round of stowing everything, the safety briefing — and lines away!

Our first destination was a cozy bay on the island of Murter. After a 1.5-hour sail, mostly under canvas, and a successful mooring to a buoy, nobody could hold back from jumping straight into the cool water. Now the vacation had truly begun! On the beach, a very nice-looking restaurant caught our eye, and the crew decided to eat there that evening — the skipper recommended reserving ahead from experience. So the dinghy went in the water, the outboard got mounted, and the first scouting party went ashore to secure a table for seven. But — unfortunately, the place was fully booked! No surprise, given how popular it is with locals and tourists alike. But our seasoned skipper just said: "Let's just try again later, once we're hungry."

After getting ready for dinner and the crew's second, successful landing on the beach, a table for seven had in fact opened up. We were served delicious appetizers, seafood, and beef carpaccio. The excellent house wine paired nicely with the main course too — a seafood platter from the local fisherman's own hand-picked catch. To finish, the waiter set out the restaurant's finest bottles of schnapps for us to sample freely.

A beautiful first day drew to a close — a quiet night under a full moon that had just risen over the glassy-clear sea.

Day 2 – The Wind Picks Up

The next day we continued south-southeast. Our destination for lunch: Tribunj!

After successfully docking in the harbor of this charming little town, the crew scattered to track down an ice cream stand. After that stop and a few errands, the day's trip continued toward Prvić, not far from Tribunj. "Raise the sails — wind's coming!" Said and done — and before we knew it, we'd already reached our original destination for the day.

"There's still wind, let's keep sailing!" No problem — new course, heading west. The wind kept building, and the crew felt real sailing joy for the first time, discovering what sailing is really about. Our close-hauled course eventually brought us into a bay on Žirje.

After a very successful sailing day, the only option there was anchoring with a line ashore. But the wind seemed to calm down by evening, making an overnight stay in this very peaceful bay ideal. Our two hobby fishermen got their rods out, while the snorkelers and sunbathers got their fill too. The meal prepared on board was the highlight of the day, which ended, like the one before, under a bright moon and glassy-clear water.

Day 3 – Off to the Kornati Islands!

Today's goal is clear — we're setting off for the Kornati islands.

Around noon, after one more round of snorkeling, we cast off. Good wind was in the forecast again today too, giving us another great day of sailing. Between the chill music, lazy sunny hours, and the relaxed mood on board, it turned into a true day of pure relaxation.

Since the first two nights were spent in bays, the crew decided to head for a marina to restock supplies. In the sparsely populated Kornati (the island group and national park), there's a popular marina called Piškera, with showers and a small market. Anyone hoping to find civilization in the Kornati, though, will be disappointed — this is a place of absolute quiet and untouched nature, far removed from markets, crowded beaches, and the usual masses this country tends to draw.

That evening, too, the crew wanted to eat out. The restaurant sits on a different small island, but its little water taxi ferries you comfortably to the jetty and back. Local specialties like lamb, raised right there on the Kornati islands, and fish were served beautifully prepared. A treat that slowly became routine, yet somehow kept impressing us more each day.

Day 4 – The Bora

The reliable Croatian weather service had already called it: the bora — a gusty northwesterly wind — was set to arrive later in the day.

The whole crew woke to raindrops tapping against the windows. The towels hung out on deck were quickly brought inside — the outer deck got a free natural cleaning courtesy of the rain, which kept starting and stopping. After breakfast, with fresh bread from the marina bakery, the crew decided to head out anyway, despite the on-and-off rain and the wind in the forecast.

Right on cue for our departure maneuver, the rain started up again — the neighboring crews watched, a bit puzzled, as our yacht slowly slipped out of the marina basin, once again setting course for Prvić, the harbor we'd left two days earlier chasing better wind. Before long, the crew was rewarded for the decision to head out: on the outer side of the Kornati, a pod of dolphins greeted us and accompanied us for a stretch before we cut through to the inner side of the islands. The rain stopped — but the wind grew noticeably stronger. After a few miles under sail it was clear: this would be a rough one. But the crew stayed upbeat: "A change of pace — nothing but sunshine would get boring anyway."

After a few rounds of setting and reefing sails, and 20 nautical miles later, the island of Prvić came into view. Thanks to the surrounding hills, the wind in the island town's harbor basin was remarkably calm — though clearly we weren't the only ones who knew that. Both the buoy field and the harbor itself were completely full. Next try — Zlarin, the island across the way. No luck there either, so we continued on via Vodice, back to Tribunj, where this time a spot in the town marina was open for us. Plenty of yachts hadn't even left their moorings that day given the weather, which left the sheltered marinas and harbors packed. The crew wanted to end the day at a proper down-to-earth restaurant — roast suckling pig with generous sides was served to the hungry crew, who'd weathered their first stormy day and were proud of it.

Day 5 – A 'Quiet' Bay

After a fairly demanding sailing day and two nights in marinas, it was clear we were headed for a bay again — a really cozy one, far from any towns, just nature.

Said and done. The skipper knew just the spot on the island of Kaprije, with a buoy field where the moderate wind forecast for the night wouldn't cause any trouble. Something rare happened for the second time this week — dolphins! Thanks to our quiet sailing course and the absence of engine noise, they stayed in sight for a while, giving the crew another chance to appreciate just how beautiful these creatures are.

After a 1.5-hour sail we'd already reached the bay. Since plenty of day-trippers had taken all the buoys, we anchored provisionally, ready to grab a buoy for the night as soon as one opened up. It didn't take long before we struck up conversation with our neighboring boats and their crews — and the evening took a completely different turn than planned. Four yacht crews of different nationalities ended up celebrating and laughing together, the whole bay caught up in that mood — a moment that happened purely by chance and could only ever be this special under those exact circumstances. Plenty of memories from that night are best left right where they belong. But as an experienced skipper, I can honestly say: I've rarely had such a fun, lighthearted evening.

Day 6 – Hangover

As I'm sure plenty of people will understand — after a night like the one just described, the next morning feels like the exact opposite. So this day started slow and easy.

In the morning, a jump into the water did wonders for getting everyone's circulation going again, and one party yacht after another said its goodbyes and left the bay. As the unofficial lead party yacht, we naturally had a duty to restock the drink supplies. Kaprije, just two nautical miles away, was the perfect spot for that — a quick stop at the little island harbor and a supply run to the local market brought our provisions back up to a satisfying level.

The day's destination — and this time, for real — was a bay for relaxing, found on the northern side of the island of Žirje. An evening of swimming and cooking on board made for another unforgettable experience, which also included one of our onboard fishermen losing his rod overboard. The reasons for that remain unclear — we can only speculate…

Day 7 – Heading Back

The second-to-last day dawned, and thanks to a local diver, the lost fishing rod was recovered after all. Today's destination: Biograd na Moru.

Even on our last day with the yacht, the crew could rely on the forecast — a maestral wind (from the northeast) was set to arrive in the afternoon. So it was clear we'd spend the morning still in the bay and the afternoon mostly sailing the open sea. Our last stop, in the bay of Murvenjak, felt like a slice of the Caribbean — and left the whole crew a little wistful that the trip was drawing to a close. After the usual refueling stop, the yacht docked that evening back in the Biograd marina, and the last night — like the first — wrapped up with a good meal and a stop or two at the local bars.
