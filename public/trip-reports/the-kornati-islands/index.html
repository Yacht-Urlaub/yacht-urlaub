The Islands of the Kornati

A cruise through Croatia's richly varied nature park

Our starting and finishing point was Biograd na Moru. We'd chosen early September for our trip and, on our skipper's recommendation, booked a Wednesday-to-Wednesday charter. That meant we could take our time getting there — no traffic jams, no marina chaos. Even so, finding a parking spot wasn't entirely straightforward. Our skipper had described exactly where to find our catamaran — a Lagoon 400 — so before we even carried our bags aboard, we ran into our sailing friends already waiting. The joy of reuniting and the anticipation of the days ahead on the water were matched only by the pleasantly warm weather and brilliant blue sky.

With the skipper, there were eight of us on board, so everyone had plenty of room for their belongings, and the provisions we'd brought along stowed away easily. Since we hadn't booked any onboard service or all-inclusive package, we each contributed an agreed amount to a shared kitty. Four of us then set off to buy drinks, fresh fruit and vegetables, and other supplies.

Once they were back and everything was stowed, the skipper ran through only the essentials, since we'd all sailed with Yacht-Urlaub before. He suggested we cast off that same day. Said and done! Everyone happily took on a task for departure, and shortly after we were leaving the marina behind.

The feeling of leaving the mainland behind and diving into the world of the Kornati islands was simply wonderful. We sailed along the island of Pašman and eventually tied up to one of the last free buoys in a bay.

Our skipper had already told us about a little restaurant, so we took the dinghy over to reserve a table for dinner. After that, nothing could stop us from diving into the crystal-clear water of the calm bay. We swam laps around the boat, simply soaking up that one-of-a-kind vacation feeling.

We spent the evening at the small island restaurant, treated to regional specialties, an excellent house wine, and, to finish, an authentic Croatian schnapps. Slightly merry and thoroughly content, we made our way back to the boat.

The next morning, it wasn't easy to leave this dreamy spot. But once the skipper had us dreaming of even more beautiful bays ahead, we finally set off after a leisurely breakfast on board. The sea was calm, but a light breeze was blowing, so for the first time we got to raise the sails. It's hard to put into words — that moment when the engine shuts off and all you hear is the wind in the sails. Simply wonderful!

Some might wonder what there is to do on board all day. There's always something to do — or nothing at all, and that's the beauty of it! Most of us spent the next stretch sunbathing on deck; a catamaran offers plenty of room for that, and the trampoline nets up front are especially relaxing. Just remember to lather up with sunscreen.

Around midday the skipper suggested dropping anchor in a bay, where we could dive straight from the boat into the sea again and swim in the crystal-clear water. We also tried stand-up paddleboarding for the first time, using a board the skipper kept on board and rented out to us for a small fee for the week.

That evening's destination was a small bay that can only be reached in certain wind conditions. It has only a handful of moorings and a restaurant that's especially popular among sailors, so we made sure to call ahead and reserve both a spot for the catamaran and a table for dinner. The house specialty is lamb roasted in a pot ("peka") with vegetables and potatoes — also something you have to order in advance, since it takes a while to prepare.

Sailing into the bay, we got an unpleasant surprise: another catamaran had simply taken our reserved mooring! But "nema problema," as they say in Croatia for good reason — there's almost always a solution, and this time was no exception. The restaurant owners were mortified by the whole situation and let us tie up instead along a wooden jetty at the side of the bay. Our skipper wasn't exactly thrilled, but between all of us we managed to get our rather large boat securely tied up. Since the forecast called for almost no wind overnight and into the morning, we were happy enough with the spot.

As it turned out, we ended up getting our welcome drink from the crew of the other catamaran instead — they may have snagged our mooring, but our ordered meal was served just the same. And it far exceeded our expectations! In short: delicious lamb, excellent wine, top-notch service — dinner among friends, an experience we won't forget.

The next morning only reinforced that feeling. The other boats had already left; only the houseboat and the restaurant owners' fishing boat remained in the bay. We waited for the sun to spill its rays over the rocks into this idyllic setting. After that, nothing could keep us aboard any longer. The early risers among us soaked in the calm, pleasantly warm seawater in this one-of-a-kind setting.

Here too, it was hard to say goodbye, but we were already looking forward to the day's destination: the harbor town of Sali on the island of Dugi Otok. It was a long way there, so everyone made themselves comfortable on board. We started out under motor, but once the wind picked up, we raised the sails. Cloudless sky, pure sunshine, blue sea, no schedule, nothing but relaxation — simply a dream.

By early afternoon we were closing in on our destination, visible from a good distance away. Sailing into the small harbor, we were glad to see there were still free spots along the quay, so we tied up there. The harbor's infrastructure gave us fresh water, electricity, and restrooms — more modest than a marina's, usually, but that didn't bother us at all. From our spot we had a great view of the town's lively bustle, and we were right in the middle of it.

Sali is the island's cultural and administrative center and its most populated town, sitting on the island's northeastern coast. Before dinner we wanted to explore the surroundings a bit more, so we set off on a walk around the harbor basin…

Naturally, we discovered a few interesting things here and there and picked up the odd souvenir. An ice cream before dinner hit the spot too. For dinner itself, the skipper led us into the town's narrow lanes, where we soon found a cozy little restaurant. The owners were delighted and set us up with a big table on the terraced steps right among the picturesque houses. We enjoyed excellent, authentically Croatian dishes and treated ourselves to some sweet desserts as well. We wrapped up the evening at the bar down at the end of the harbor.

The next morning we went shopping first — fresh bread from the little bakery around the corner, fruit and vegetables at the market next door, and everything else at the supermarket across the way. Then we enjoyed a big breakfast on board. There was always someone happy to cook, and it's a lot of fun doing it together — plus we had an enthusiastic chef among us who kept spoiling us with delicious meals.

Before moving on, we enjoyed one more walk on a ridge trail outside of town, which gave us a gorgeous view over the neighboring islands. With a bit of wistfulness, we said goodbye to Sali.

That day we tackled our longest stretch yet, aiming for Molat. The sky once again showed its best side, though the wind kept us waiting, so we only made partial progress under sail. In the early afternoon we headed for a swimming bay, which — thanks to a strong current — turned out to be a bit of a challenge for us swimmers. That's where the stand-up paddleboard came back into play, and the skipper showed us just how fast and effortless it can be, especially riding with the current.

As soon as we reached the bay at Molat, we knew right away the long haul had been worth it. We were one of the few boats tied to a buoy there that Saturday evening. The swimmers among us were straight back in the sea, enjoying the calm and, once again, crystal-clear water.

Meanwhile, the skipper reserved us a table at one of the konobas — as the local restaurants are called in Croatia — lining the nearby shore. This time the owner even came to pick us up in his own fishing boat. From the restaurant's terrace we got to watch a gorgeous sunset before dinner was served. Everyone ordered something different off the à la carte menu, so we got to admire — or even sample — all sorts of regional specialties across the table. Restaurant owners always appreciate it when the bill gets settled as one, so we'd agreed in advance to pay for meals ashore out of the shared kitty too. It worked beautifully the whole trip. This evening, once again, gave us something special, and one we're sure to remember.

Early the next morning — a Sunday — the bay showed itself in all its beauty, and the thought "what a wonderful world God created" stayed with us from then on. Swimming was the first order of business for us early risers, and this time it was especially hard to climb back out of the water — it was simply paradise here.

But our skipper had one more highlight in store — a bay he'd heard plenty about but had never actually visited himself. Since we hadn't fixed a set route at the start of the trip and had simply put our trust in the skipper, we sailed a bit further north on his recommendation. The weather had now been glorious for five straight days, so sunbathing on deck and in the catamaran's trampoline "hammock" during the crossing was pure pleasure. And the anticipation of a new, idyllic swimming bay only made it sweeter.

The sight of the bay exceeded every expectation. It wasn't exactly secluded — there was a beach club there, a very picturesque one with a roped-off swimming area — but the color of the water alone made us think we'd landed in a Caribbean bay. We all agreed: a slice of paradise!

That Sunday afternoon was a true highlight of the trip, and I think we spent most of it in the water. There were hardly any other boats around, so this oversized "swimming pool" was almost entirely ours.

By evening, we reluctantly said goodbye to this extraordinary bay to spend the night in the marina of a small fishing village. Despite the off-season, plenty of sailboats had gathered here — and the village cats seemed to already be expecting them. Countless cats lounged along the docks, clearly hoping for a handout.

We, too, were looking forward to dinner and found a restaurant known for its steaks. Tonight we felt like eating meat, after living mostly off fresh seafood and fish the past few days. Luckily we still found a table for eight, which is often tricky without a reservation. The food was good, but we agreed we'd eaten better on this trip.

The next morning, for the first time, we were genuinely glad to be moving on. Today's destination was Kornati National Park, a special sailing area you're only allowed to enter after paying a fee. The alternative is booking ahead at one of the handful of restaurants there to secure a mooring — which our skipper had managed after several attempts.

So we sailed in from the north through the narrow passage into Kornati National Park. It's quite a sight watching boats file through this "needle's eye," one after another, mindful of oncoming traffic.

The Kornati islands form the densest island cluster in the Mediterranean and were placed under strict nature protection as a national park in 1980. The scenery here is one of a kind, with an atmosphere all its own. A few of us had been here before, but everyone agreed they're captivated all over again every time.

After cruising slowly past the Kornati's gentle rolling hills, we reached our destination for the day: a small but well-known restaurant with its own short jetty. Since it's generally standard practice among boaters to help each other tie up, we were surprised when the owner of the motorboat already at the dock made no move to help — and instead brusquely asked whether we were even allowed to moor there at all. One of us had to jump ashore quickly to secure the first line. Thankfully, you don't run into people like that very often.

Since it was still only early afternoon, everyone got to pursue their own particular passion — some were straight back in (or under) the water, others admired the fascinating plant life, and still others climbed the hills to soak in the spectacular views.

Before heading to the small konoba for dinner, we soaked up the Croatian islands' unique sunset atmosphere one more time. The next morning made saying goodbye — and heading back to the marina in Biograd — especially hard. The view from our boat was simply gorgeous, and a swim in the sea only reinforced what we were all thinking: "We'll be back!"
