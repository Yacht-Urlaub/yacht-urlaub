The Dodecanese

A Fascinating Journey Through the Islands of the Aegean

"Chasing the sunrise" feels like the right way to describe how our trip begins. We head to the airport in the very early morning, driving east just as the sun comes up. A good omen, I think to myself, still a little sleepy but already looking forward to the sailing trip ahead.

At the airport we meet our fellow sailors and our skipper, Manuel. Six of us in total. We fly together to the island of Kos, our starting point for the trip. Everything goes smoothly, and we land at Kos airport around midday. Even though the season is only just getting started, there's already quite a bit going on here in late May. Our taxi driver is waiting for us right at the airport, so we're able to continue on without any delay. Before long we reach our destination — the marina in Kos.

Day 1

Our sailing catamaran — a Lagoon 380 — is already waiting when our skipper invites us aboard. She's not the newest boat around, but she's in great shape and clearly well cared for, and we feel right at home from the first minute. Three of us take a rental car to pick up the last of the provisions for the galley. Once they're back, we stow the luggage, food, and drinks and are soon ready to cast off. Since everyone in the crew already has some sailing experience, the skipper's briefing doesn't take long, and we're off. We leave Kos marina behind and set a course for our first stop of the day.

With the Turkish coast in view, we sail north and soon reach the small island of Psérimos. We find a spot along the short quay and drop anchor for the first time. We've arrived! The little village greets us with an unspoiled, authentically Greek way of life, and the handful of tourists around this evening are almost all sailors. At the restaurant we pick for dinner, we feel at home right away. We order a mix of dishes the owner recommends and get to enjoy some genuinely good, traditional cooking. As the evening goes on, the place fills up, and we're happy to see it's almost entirely locals — always a good sign.

Day 2

After a first breakfast aboard the next morning, we decide to make the most of what the island has to offer — starting with its beautiful sandy beach. At this early hour, with the water still on the cool side, I'm apparently the only one tempted for a swim, but I enjoy every minute of it while the others go shopping. The island is also known for its sponge divers, and it turns out it's nearly impossible to leave without a sponge of your own.

At one of the stalls, we pick up a set of shot glasses, since we're missing them aboard for the traditional "arrival toast." While we're at it, we learn that most locals here really only stay through the summer, and outside of the season the island's population drops to just a handful of people. The ferry pulls in while we're there, bringing back a few residents who'd clearly been running errands on one of the bigger islands. And right as we're casting off, the first day-tripper boat arrives — the island's quiet little spell already broken for the day.

Our goal for day two is a bay on the island of Kálymnos. The wind is light, but it's coming from the right direction, so we raise the sails and let it carry us along. We reach our destination shortly after midday. There are two large fields of mooring buoys to choose from, and picking the right one matters, since the taverna waiting for us that evening depends on it. We go with gut instinct, and it pays off later.

Our onboard "chefs," Harald and Sabine, really get going for the first time and show us just what a small galley kitchen is capable of. They put together a baked feta — feta fourno — for lunch with real care, and it doesn't just look good, it tastes fantastic too. The temperatures, by the way, are perfectly pleasant, and we all settle in to enjoy a relaxed afternoon on board.

Later, three of us take the dinghy to a small cove with a pebble beach nearby. The water is crystal clear, making for some truly great swimming and snorkeling. Once we're back, it's time to get ready for dinner. With two heads with showers plus an outdoor shower, the six of us are well taken care of, so it doesn't take long before the skipper is running us ashore in the dinghy. From the restaurant, we have a gorgeous view over the whole bay — our catamaran anchored right in the middle of it — and we're already looking forward to sampling the Greek specialties on offer here.

Day 3

Today's destination is the island of Léros, or more precisely the small fishing harbor of Agia Marina. Wind is moderate again today, so we motor-sail part of the way, taking it easy and just enjoying life. There aren't many other boats out, which makes it entertaining to watch how the few sailors we do see handle the wind. Arriving in the harbor, we're not quite sure where to tie up until a fisherman heading out gives us a pointer. As we come alongside the harbor wall right in front of a good-sized fishing boat, we get an unexpected hand from a group of Bavarian sailors anchored in the opposite bay, who happen to be setting off on foot to explore the island's fortress. We invite them over for an arrival toast, which turns into a lively conversation between sailors. Not long after, we head off ourselves to explore the picturesque village and track down a good taverna.

Day 4

The next morning we witness something that's probably an everyday occurrence in Greece. A coast guard boat has picked up a group of refugees and is bringing them ashore — four young men, along with some women and a small child. The harbor area is cordoned off, and everything unfolds calmly. After a while, a minibus arrives and takes everyone away. The scene stays with us, and it drives home just how challenging the situation is for countries sitting on the EU's external border.

We say goodbye to Léros and head toward the island of Lipsí without a fixed destination in mind. We want to spend this fourth day hopping between bays and really taking in the scenery the Dodecanese have to offer. An early highlight is a small, oddly shaped island whose rock formations and emerald-green water make for something really special.

With genuinely good wind now, we sail on and tuck into a hidden anchorage on Lipsí late in the afternoon. To make sure we spend a calm night here, the skipper insists on a line ashore, which he and I secure to the rocks. In the end we even drop a second anchor. The recurring strong gusts aren't the only surprise this little bay has in store — one of our crew loses their phone while taking photos. We can see it clearly resting on the seabed right next to the boat, but no amount of snorkeling or improvised gear can retrieve it; it's simply too deep at this spot.

To make up for it, our onboard cooks treat us to something special that evening: pan-fried fish, bought fresh that morning straight off the boats in Agia Marina. Between the atmosphere and the view over the bay — which we have entirely to ourselves — every worry quickly fades away. Once again, we're reminded: this is exactly what a Yacht-Urlaub vacation is all about.

Day 5

Today we have a special destination in mind: the island of Patmos, home to the world-famous 11th-century Monastery of St. John. But first we want to anchor in a bay for a swim. When we arrive, we're struck by the shades of blue in the water and the bay's almost Caribbean feel. It's simply stunning, and swimming here is a one-of-a-kind pleasure.

Since our skipper thinks we shouldn't leave for Patmos too late, we reluctantly say goodbye to this idyllic spot and sail northwest. From a distance, we can already make out the monastery and the old town of Chora, perched on the hill above the harbor town of Skala. Since it's still early afternoon, we easily find a spot along the harbor wall in Patmos's main town.

Four of us decide to head up to the monastery hill that same day, just to get a first look at this nearly thousand-year-old complex. We know the monastery itself is only open for visits in the morning, but the old town's narrow lanes should be especially charming in the early evening, without the usual crowds. We take a taxi up the hill, and once we're at the top, we're glad we did. Never underestimate elevation gain just because you're by the sea!

We turn out to be the only tourists around. Just one shop is still open, and its bright colors pull us in like a magnet — "us" meaning the two women in the group, while the men wander on ahead. Naturally, we walk out with the perfect souvenir.

Wandering the steep but, at this hour, pleasantly shaded and cool lanes of Chora's old town around the Monastery of St. John is another highlight of the trip.

We round off the evening together at a classic Greek ouzeri.

Day 6

The day starts early for three of us, since we've agreed to be at the monastery right when it opens. This time we hitch a ride on a packed school bus to get up the hill. The view down to the sea that morning is breathtaking, and it's impossible not to feel humbled by the beauty of the landscape.

We're among the first visitors to the Monastery of St. John that morning. It's genuinely fascinating to take in so many culturally significant treasures spanning ten centuries. The oldest part of the monastery left the biggest impression on me — its inner courtyard and small church, adorned with large, intricately crafted icons that stand as moving testaments to centuries of Christian faith.

We leave Patmos around midday, aiming to reach our next stop, a bay on Kálymnos, by late afternoon. With moderate wind, we sail south now, since our trip is slowly winding down — though we'd rather not think about that and instead stay curious about what's still ahead. And we're not disappointed. The bay where we drop anchor for the night is stunning: tall cliffs ring a small village that clings picturesquely to the steep rock face. We're thrilled, and we soak up every bit of the evening atmosphere.

We take the dinghy ashore once more for dinner, and once again we find what we've discovered again and again on this trip: that authentic, unspoiled Greece — far from mass tourism — still exists. You just have to get away from the tourist centers to find it, and there's really no better way to do that than aboard a sailing yacht, free to drop anchor in whatever dream bay you like.

Day 7

Today, unfortunately, it's time to say goodbye to the fascinating islands of the Dodecanese and sail back to the marina in Kos. None of us can quite believe we'll be leaving the boat again tomorrow morning, and the mood is a little subdued because of it. The wind, at least, picks up, giving us one last chance to sail to our hearts' content...
