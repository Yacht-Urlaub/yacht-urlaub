Split to Dubrovnik

A journey along Croatia's southern coast

Day 1, Saturday – Exploring Split

Our home port was Split. Since a good part of the crew had already arrived overnight on Friday, they got to enjoy the following day to the fullest: Split, southern Dalmatia's capital and a UNESCO World Heritage city, offers plenty to see. Diocletian's Palace, the Cathedral of St. Domnius, and the Riva promenade are just a few of the highlights. The regular market offers regional delicacies plus fresh fruit and vegetables. The crew decided to stock up on fresh provisions there. The yacht handover was scheduled for the afternoon. The rest of the crew arrived and the boat was handed over around 3 p.m. Moving in and stowing the groceries was done in no time.

We were ready to cast off around 4 p.m. when a rumble of thunder rolled in. It was quickly clear a storm was moving in, so the obvious call was to push our departure to the next day and find a restaurant in Split instead. We sat out the storm comfortably in a restaurant, enjoying excellent food. With a meal that good, the last-minute change of plans was forgotten fast.

Day 2, Sunday – Let's Go!

Institute of Oceanography and Fisheries — passed on the way out of Kaštela Bay, with the imposing Dinaric Alps in the background

The next day the crew was up relatively early to set sail toward Dubrovnik — an ambitious goal for a single week, since we also had to leave time for the return trip to our home port in Split.

Breakfast wasn't served until after we'd cast off and were underway, which — with the sea this calm — was very welcome. Over fresh bread and hot coffee, the first comment of the day, from one of the female crew members, was: "I never imagined it could be this gorgeous." We set a course through the Hvar channel, then continued south-southeast toward Korčula, our destination for the day. As if on cue, a light breeze picked up and we raised the sails, making it all the way to the Hvar channel on wind power alone.

Then something unexpected happened. A crew member noticed the fuel gauge needle jumping strangely back and forth, which caught the skipper's attention, since the engine is normally off while sailing. An attempt to start the engine failed. With the wind this light, reaching Korčula under sail alone was no longer realistic for the day. The local service team, always ready to help, arrived quickly: heavy rain the day before had caused water damage to the engine's start panel, and the technicians swapped out the control unit on the spot. While that was underway, part of the crew got to explore the town of Hvar, while others took their first plunge into the swimming cove across the way. The panel swap was finished by late afternoon, and the crew decided to wrap up the day right there in the nearby marina — with an excellent dinner at one of the country's best restaurants.

Day 3, Monday – Eyes on the Prize

That morning the crew agreed: today we needed to cover real distance. We cast off in the late morning, once again bound for Korčula.

The day turned into a relaxed one on board — music, plenty of reading, and sunbathing. We passed the striking town of Hvar once more, and a little later sailed through the Korčula channel. The mountains on the opposite shore rise to nearly 1,000 meters and look impressive from every angle. The day was already getting on when a cozy bay on the island of Mljet, inside a small national park, sounded irresistible as our next stop. We managed to tie up to a buoy — owned by a local restaurant — just before 6 p.m.

Swimming and snorkeling in this gorgeous bay: an experience like no other!

Day 4, Tuesday – Dubrovnik, a World Heritage City

Since we'd already covered a long stretch the day before, we could take Tuesday at a more leisurely pace. There was a hearty breakfast with fresh pastries from the local bakery. Then we said goodbye to our idyllic bay and set off again toward Dubrovnik.

Alternating between sail and motor, we reached the old city walls of Dubrovnik. It was a bit disheartening to see a massive cruise ship anchored offshore, with swarms of little water taxis ferrying cruise passengers into town.

We turned our backs on that scene and continued past Dubrovnik's main harbor, where another cruise ship passed us on its way out. We set course for the Dubrovnik marina, which sits a little outside town and offers excellent restrooms and showers, a swimming pool, and great shopping infrastructure.

A bus or taxi gets you comfortably into the Old Town from there. Walking the ancient walls that held off every enemy throughout history, or strolling the narrow lanes past countless restaurants and galleries, is an experience in its own right.

Proud to have reached our destination, we wrapped up a successful day with a few relaxed drinks before retreating to our bunks on the yacht. It's safe to say: Dubrovnik is a destination worth every mile.

Day 5, Wednesday – From City to Wilderness

Part of the crew decided to head back into Dubrovnik's culture-rich Old Town early the next morning. The rest used the time to sleep in and enjoy a long breakfast. As a result, we didn't leave the marina until around noon — with a new destination in mind: Lastovo, an island that sits a bit off on its own in the Adriatic but fit our route nicely. It, too, is part of a national park and completely unspoiled — not a tourist in sight. And the water — a dream! Pristine quality, as you'd expect. In Croatia that's pretty much a given, but it's worth mentioning again here.

Day 6, Thursday – Vis

Our destination on the second-to-last day at sea was the town of Komiža on the island of Vis. It sits tucked away in a bay ringed by high mountains, and the approach from the south is as scenic as it is interesting. The well-known fishing village of Komiža itself has a town harbor and several small shops and restaurants. A good restaurant sits right on the water, where a cool evening breeze was more than welcome. A charming little place, and one the crew agreed was a fitting spot for our last evening before heading home.

Day 7, Friday – The Blue Grotto

On our last day at sea, it was already clear by morning that a moderate breeze was building. Since Komiža sits across from the small island of Biševo, a visit to the Blue Grotto was an obvious choice — a sea cave you can reach by dinghy, putting on a remarkable display of color in the morning hours. After a memorable visit, it was time for the sail back to Split. Luckily we managed to cover the entire stretch under sail. A swim stop along the way at Brač — in a bay near Milna — gave everyone a chance to slowly say goodbye to the sea. Once we'd docked at our home port, the crew decided on one more exploration of Split's Old Town. Proud and full of impressions from the week, we had dinner at one of the excellent restaurants in downtown Split. And so a beautiful, wonderfully varied sailing holiday came to an end.
